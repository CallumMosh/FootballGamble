Methodology

How HitRate actually works

The reasoning behind every read, laid out plainly — because "trust us" isn't good enough.

The core idea

HitRate doesn't predict the future. Every read is descriptive — a plain-English summary of what's actually happened in a team's recent games, not a forecast dressed up as one. If a team has scored in 5 of its last 6 games, that's a fact about the last 6 games. It's not a promise about the next one.

Every stat on the site lists the actual games behind it. Nothing is a black-box number — tap any read and you can check the working yourself.

The 6-game window

Most reads are built from each team's last 6 finished matches. Six is a deliberate trade-off: long enough to smooth out a single freak result, short enough to actually reflect current form rather than form from four months ago. We don't stretch the window to manufacture a bigger sample — if a team hasn't played 6 games yet, the read simply isn't shown.

Early in a season, this means some teams won't have reads yet. That's intentional — a confident-looking stat built on 2 games would be more misleading than no stat at all.

One competition at a time

We never compare stats across different competitions as if they're on the same scale. A team's goals-per-game in the Bundesliga isn't comparable to a team's goals-per-game in Ligue 1 — the opposition is different, the styles are different, the numbers just don't mean the same thing. Every read stays inside a single competition, even when that means a smaller sample.

Who Wins

The head-to-favourite read blends three signals: each team's recent form, their league position, and the head-to-head record between them. We won't publish the exact weighting — not because it's complicated, but because it's the one part of the site simplest to copy wholesale, and it's tuned from real mistakes we've caught and fixed over time.

What we will say: recent form carries the most weight. League position only counts when there's a genuine, settled table behind it — not an early-season table or a group-stage standing that can't yet mean much. Head-to-head only counts once there's a meaningful number of past meetings to draw on; a single past result doesn't move the read.

If form and league position point in sharply opposite directions, we don't just let them cancel out quietly — the read is flagged as genuinely hard to call rather than presented with false confidence.

How other markets are built

Beyond Who Wins, every market on the site is built one of three honest ways:

  • Pooled — for markets like Over 2.5 Goals or BTTS, we combine both teams' actual game-by-game history and check the real hit rate across all of it. We never multiply two separate percentages together to fake a combined number (e.g. "win rate × clean sheet rate" for win-to-nil) — that produces a number that looks plausible but isn't real. We always check the actual joint outcome, game by game.
  • Opponent-tempered — for markets like "to score" or "clean sheet," a team's own rate is blended with how their opponent tends to perform at the other end. A team that scores often against weak defences reads differently against a strong one.
  • Team-only — where there's no sensible opponent factor to apply, we just show the team's own rate, plainly, with no adjustment pretending otherwise.

Track Record

This is the honesty check. We backtest each read type against real results the same teams already produced — including the misses, not just the hits. A market needs at least 7 finished games within a single competition before it's eligible, so early in a season, or partway through a shorter tournament, this section can legitimately be empty. We don't lower that bar to make the page look more populated — six games means six games, no exceptions for a knockout competition that hasn't produced enough of them yet.

What we deliberately don't cover

Some popular betting markets aren't on HitRate at all: first goalscorer, exact correct score, corners, cards, in-game/live markets. That's not an oversight — our data source doesn't provide reliable numbers for these on the tier we use, and we'd rather leave a market off entirely than fill it with a thin or unreliable read.
